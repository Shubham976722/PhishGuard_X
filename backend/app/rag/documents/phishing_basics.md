# Phishing Basics

## What is phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering attack in which an attacker
pretends to be a trusted person or organization to trick a
victim into revealing sensitive information or performing
an unsafe action.

Common targets include passwords, banking credentials,
payment information, and personal data.

## How phishing URLs work

Attackers create URLs that imitate legitimate websites.
They may use misleading subdomains, misspelled brand names,
unusual domain extensions, URL shorteners, or deceptive
paths to make a link appear trustworthy.

A phishing URL may lead to a fake login page that collects
credentials or to a page that attempts to deliver malware.

## Common warning signs

- Misspelled or unusual domain names.
- Unexpected requests for passwords or financial details.
- URLs containing misleading brand names.
- Unsolicited messages creating urgency.
- Unexpected attachments or shortened links.
- Domains that imitate, but do not exactly match, legitimate
  organizations.

## HTTPS and phishing

HTTPS encrypts communication between a browser and a website.
It does not prove that a website is legitimate or trustworthy.
Phishing websites can also use HTTPS certificates.

## How to respond to a suspicious URL

- Do not enter credentials or payment information.
- Do not download unexpected files.
- Verify the domain using the organization's official website.
- Report suspicious messages to the relevant organization.
- If credentials were entered, change the affected password
  using the official website and enable multifactor
  authentication.

## Important limitation

URL-based indicators are signals, not proof of malicious
activity. A URL should not be declared safe solely because
it uses HTTPS or has a familiar-looking domain.
