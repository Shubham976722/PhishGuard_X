## 1. What Is Phishing?

Phishing is a form of social engineering in which attackers impersonate trusted people or organizations to trick users into revealing sensitive information, opening malicious links, downloading harmful attachments, or performing actions that benefit the attacker.

Phishing can occur through:
- Email
- SMS and text messages
- Social media messages
- Phone calls
- Fake websites
- Other digital communication channels

Attackers commonly attempt to steal usernames, passwords, financial information, and other personal data.

## 2. Common Phishing Warning Signs

Users should look for the following warning signs:

- Urgent messages demanding immediate action.
- Requests for passwords, banking information, or personal details.
- Links that do not match the organization they claim to represent.
- Unexpected attachments.
- Suspicious sender addresses or domains.
- Untrusted shortened URLs.
- Unexpected requests to verify or unlock accounts.
- Offers that appear unusually attractive or unrealistic.

Modern phishing messages may use correct grammar and professional language. Poor spelling is not a reliable requirement for identifying phishing.

## 3. Social Engineering Techniques

Attackers use psychological manipulation to influence victims.

Common techniques include:

### Urgency

Attackers claim an account will be suspended or a payment will fail unless the victim acts immediately.

### Fear

Messages may claim that suspicious activity has occurred and that the victim must provide information to secure an account.

### Authority Impersonation

Attackers pretend to represent banks, government agencies, employers, or well-known companies.

### Financial Incentives

Fake refunds, prizes, discounts, and investment opportunities encourage victims to click malicious links or disclose information.

### Familiarity and Trust

Attackers impersonate colleagues, friends, service providers, or organizations that the victim already knows.

## 4. How to Respond to Suspected Phishing

If a message appears suspicious:

1. Do not click its links.
2. Do not open unexpected attachments.
3. Do not provide passwords or financial information.
4. Verify the request through an independently obtained official contact method.
5. Report the suspicious message using the appropriate reporting channel.
6. Delete the message if it is confirmed to be malicious.

Never use the phone number or contact details inside a suspicious message to verify its legitimacy.

## 5. Protecting Accounts

Recommended security practices include:

- Use unique and strong passwords.
- Use a password manager.
- Enable multi-factor authentication.
- Prefer phishing-resistant authentication methods when available.
- Keep operating systems and applications updated.
- Use available email filtering and security protections.
- Report suspected phishing incidents promptly.

Multi-factor authentication can reduce account compromise risk, but some MFA methods can still be targeted by phishing attacks.

## 6. What to Do After Falling Victim

If credentials were submitted to a suspected phishing website:

1. Change the affected password immediately using the official website.
2. Change the same password anywhere else it was reused.
3. Enable or review multi-factor authentication.
4. Contact the affected organization or financial institution.
5. Review account activity for unauthorized access.
6. Report the incident through the appropriate organizational or government channel.

If financial information was exposed, contact the relevant bank or payment provider immediately.

## 7. Important Security Limitations

A suspicious message is not automatically proof of phishing.

Likewise, a professional-looking message is not proof of legitimacy.

Security decisions should consider multiple signals, independent verification, and available threat intelligence rather than relying on a single indicator.
